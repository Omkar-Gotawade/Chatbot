Diagnostic Scope

v1.2 Coverage
  • Power & Boot Failures: POST codes, PSU power switches, wall socket load tests, and power button continuity.
  • Video & Display Pipelines: Cable handshake, integrated vs. discrete GPU outputs, and monitor input sources.
  • Thermals & Throttling: Heatsink seating, dust accumulation, thermal paste degradation, and fan bearing noise.
  • Peripherals & Buses: USB power draw, controller resets, Wi-Fi driver handshakes, and battery cycle integrity.

Mandatory Safety Rule: Always disconnect mains AC power and depress the power button for 10 seconds before opening any computer chassis. If you observe burning odor, smoke, or physical bulging of capacitors/batteries, do not power on the machine.

Diagnostic Methodology

How Structured Troubleshooting Works

Hardware issues are diagnosed from the easiest, lowest-risk external factors down to internal modular components.

1

Isolate External Factors

Rule out wall outlets, faulty surge protectors, loose power cords, and conflicting USB peripherals before investigating internal hardware.

2

Identify POST & LED Signals

Motherboards communicate failures via acoustic beep codes, 7-segment debug codes, or diagnostic LEDs for CPU, DRAM, VGA, and Boot.

3

Perform Minimum POST Test

Strip the system down to core essentials (motherboard, 1 RAM stick, CPU, and PSU) to isolate faulty secondary components.

4

Determine Escalation Threshold

Know when an issue requires specialized equipment like multimeters, soldering irons, or warranty RMA rather than home repair.

Service Guidelines

When to Cease Self-Troubleshooting

Safety and data integrity always take precedence over manual diagnosis.

Immediate Danger Signs

Immediately disconnect mains power if you experience:

  • Acrid electrical odor or visible smoke emitting from the power supply unit.
  • Swollen or expanding lithium-ion laptop batteries (severe fire hazard).
  • Sparks during power switch actuation or audible popping noises.
  • Liquid spills on energized internal circuits.

Professional Repair Criteria

Seek qualified workbench service or warranty support when:

  • Diagnosis isolates the failure to soldered surface-mount motherboard components.
  • Power Supply Unit (PSU) internals are suspect — never open a PSU case yourself.
  • Laptop display ribbon cables requires panel disassembly under active warranty.
  • Storage drive mechanical head failure threatens permanent data loss.
